# 04 - Deals

## Emerging Buy Box Philosophy

The project has deliberately resisted forcing a narrow acquisition niche too early. Candidate categories: RV parks, car washes, laundromats, self storage, mobile home parks, service businesses, owner-operated companies, and other opportunities where operational improvement and creative structures can create value.

The eventual buy box should balance economics and founder fit. Key questions:

- Does the business reward systems thinking?
- Is there recurring revenue?
- How owner-dependent is it?
- Does Kyle want to operate it or stay strategic?
- What are the geographic constraints?
- What capital/credit does it require?
- Are the underlying problems ones Kyle actually enjoys solving?

Principle: the first acquisition doesn't need to define the career. The first dozens of owner conversations can be treated as pattern-building research.

## Potential Deal Structures

Seller financing, subject-to arrangements, partnerships, management agreements, master leases, equity splits, referrals, and other creative structures — matched to the owner's real constraint rather than a default transaction type.

## RV Park Battle Royale

An RV-park research database built for a Zero Down challenge, focused on parks within roughly a two-hour radius of Paducah. Kentucky-only, 81 parks, 31 Priority A. Built Aug 20 2026; workbook `Keldrin_RV_Parks_KY_BattleRoyale.xlsx` and Google Sheet "Keldrin - RV Park Battle Royale - Kentucky".

Scoring: Owner Signal (30) / Digital Footprint (20) / Scale (20) / Low Attention (15) / Proximity (15). Bands A ≥ 82, B 68-81, C < 68. Owner first names were pulled out of Google reviews wherever guests named them — that column is the most useful thing in the sheet and no directory will ever supply it.

Planned next step (not yet started): expand this dataset from Kentucky-only to the same four-state (KY/IL/MO/TN), two-hour-drive-time radius as the Storage Unit and Mobile Home Park datasets below, so all three sourcing lists share one consistent geography.

## Paducah AI & Energy Campus — External Catalyst (added Aug 26 2026)

DOE announced July 29 2026 that Brookfield (developer) and NextEra Energy (power) will build a $100B+ hyperscale AI data center campus on 600 leased acres of the former Paducah Gaseous Diffusion Plant (McCracken County, near Kevil) — 1.8 GW data center capacity, backed by a dedicated 2 GW gas plant + up to 2.6 GW battery storage. ~8,000 construction jobs, ~600 permanent jobs. Phased construction 2026–2031, first components live 2028. McCracken County was already an estimated 2,000–5,000 housing units short before this announcement; county is actively rezoning, expediting permits, and extending utilities in response. Local RV parks (Adams Acres) already expect to fill within a year purely from early DOE-site workforce.

This directly reinforces the existing storage/RV/MHP thesis rather than replacing it — the fragmented, mostly-unwebsited ownership base across all three sourcing datasets is poorly positioned to respond quickly to the coming demand, which is the opening. Notably, **On Track Storage in Kevil** — already in the Storage Unit Battle Royale dataset, same ownership (270-443-1511) as AAA Stowaway in Paducah — sits inside the immediate footprint of the announced campus and is worth a call regardless of anything else. Kevil itself also has two mobile home parks in the new dataset (Boone Crossing Community and a park at 8353 Paducah Rd with a directly identified owner, Steve Sullivan) worth an early drive-by given the same proximity.

A full investor/partner-facing PDF brief was built covering the project, timeline, regional population/housing context, and market-impact analysis across self-storage, RV/mobile home parks, and workforce housing, plus an infrastructure and "reading the window" section. Delivered to Kyle Aug 26 2026 as `Keldrin_Paducah_AI_Campus_Market_Brief.pdf` (12 pages, charcoal/gold branded, real Keldrin K-mark on the cover, source HTML built with Playwright/Chromium PDF rendering — not saved to this project as a binary; regenerate from scratch if a refreshed version is needed, since underlying facts like PSC approval status and final tax structure are still unresolved and may move). Revised same day per Kyle's feedback: the real Keldrin logo replaced a placeholder "K" glyph and the cover tagline was shortened to "Creative Business Acquisitions"; every storage/RV Park figure in the brief was re-scoped from the full 4-state/2-hour dataset down to just the subset within a 30-45 minute drive of the Kevil site (64 self-storage facilities, 40 RV parks — down from 269 and 81) since the original figures didn't fit a brief scoped to one specific site; and pages 4 and 13, which had overflowed onto extra pages, were condensed (mostly tightened layout, one merged fact-table row, minor prose trims) back down to one page each, taking the brief from 14 pages to 12.

National precedent worth remembering: institutional workforce-housing capital (Target Hospitality's "Target HyperScale," Civeo/Corporate Mobile Housing, DataCenterPads) typically arrives at hyperscale data center sites 12–24 months after public confirmation, once labor headcounts firm up — i.e., roughly 2027–2028 for Paducah. That's the rough window before independent RV park, storage, and MHP owner conversations get harder/more expensive.
