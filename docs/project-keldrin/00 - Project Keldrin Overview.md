## Executive Snapshot

Keldrin LLC is a newly formed Kentucky company being built as a flexible platform for creative business acquisitions, ownership, operations, and long-term value creation. The founder is Kyle Brandon Hoppmann, commonly known as Hopp.

The business began through Kyle's participation in the Zero Down community associated with Pace Morby and his team. The near-term goal is not to pretend to be a mature private-equity firm; it is to build credibility, relationships, deal skills, systems, and a professional identity while learning creative acquisition structures and collaborating with more experienced operators.

## Founder Profile & Operating Style

Kyle's core theme isn't "wanting to buy businesses" — it's genuine enjoyment of solving problems, recognizing patterns, improving systems, and listening to people. That lens drives the brand and acquisition strategy.

- Problem solver, pattern-recognizer, systems thinker, optimizer
- Listener over salesperson — prefers discovery and curiosity to high-pressure persuasion
- Integrator mindset — connects people, information, tools, and processes into workable solutions
- Learning style: unconventional explanations and practical systems fit his mental model better than standard instruction (see the Rubik's Cube story in [[01 - Brand DNA]])

## Why Keldrin Exists

Keldrin should not lead with "we buy businesses." The broader concept is solving ownership problems — an acquisition is one possible solution, not the only one. Structures under consideration: seller financing, subject-to arrangements, partnerships, management agreements, master leases, equity splits, referrals.

**Working vision:** "To become the first call business owners make when they don't know what to do next."

**Working mission:** Solve ownership problems by listening first, thinking creatively, and building solutions that benefit the people involved.

A central personal motivation is building something that provides for Kyle and the people he loves, especially his parents as they enter retirement.

## Messaging Guardrails (apply everywhere)

- Do not describe Keldrin as an established PE firm, fund, institutional investor, or large acquisition team
- Do not claim completed acquisitions, portfolio companies, AUM, proprietary capital, or financing capacity unless Kyle supplies evidence
- Do not imply Kyle is using substantial personal capital or credit — early deals rely on creative structures, community relationships, and partners
- Prefer "acquisitions," "creative business acquisitions," "ownership transitions," "problem solving," "long-term value creation" over hype-heavy investment language
- Keep Kyle approachable — professional doesn't mean stiff or corporate
- Avoid guru language, exaggerated income claims, hustle clichés, artificial certainty
- Lead with listening when talking to owners, not "we buy businesses"
- Don't lock into a single vertical — the buy box is intentionally still being developed
- Keep a clean separation between the public brand and private/sensitive legal, banking, tax, or personal info
